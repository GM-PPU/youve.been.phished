# Nice try, curious human! 🧐

## This one is a training prop. A real unknown QR might not be so friendly.

## 🚩 Red Flags Before You Scan

⚠ Sticker placed over another QR

⚠ URL has misspellings or odd domains (.ru, .xyz)

⚠ Urgency: "act now," "expires today"

⚠ Unexpected QR in an email or text

⚠ Asks for login / payment immediately after scan

⚠ Code in an unsecured public location

⚠ No printed URL to verify alongside it

⚠ App download prompt right after scanning

## ✅ Best Practices

1. Preview the URL before opening it. Your phone shows the destination before you tap "Open." Read it — does it match the brand?
2. Check for tampering. Does the QR look like a sticker over something else? Peeling edges, misalignment, or a slightly raised surface are signs.
3. Never enter credentials right after scanning. If a QR sends you directly to a login page — stop. Go to the site directly via browser instead.
4. Use your camera app, not a random QR scanner app. Third-party QR apps can log your scans and redirect URLs silently.
5. When in doubt, type the URL manually. If you know the destination (a restaurant menu, company portal), just type it.
6. Report suspicious QR codes to IT. Found a strange sticker on office equipment or a poster in the lobby? Tell IT before others scan it.

## ⭐ Golden Rules

- Preview the URL — if it looks wrong, don't open it.
- A QR code asking for your login right away is almost always a phish.
- Check for stickers — attackers cover real codes with fake ones.
- Never download an app from a QR code you didn't expect.
- When in doubt, type the URL — don't scan.
